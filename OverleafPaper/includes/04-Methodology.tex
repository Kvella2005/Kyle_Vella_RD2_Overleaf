\section{Research Methodology}
\label{sec:Res}

\par The zero shot classifier will use the big five personality framework to score based on user's open response. A questions related to Big-Five personality framework were asked to users. User's input was collected and scored with a specific big-five trait. Data would be collected based on scores scored by the ZSC and time taken to score, A comparative analysis would be conducted scores both by ZSC and human reviewers. A Pearson Correlation would be used to measure strength between AI and human scores.  

\par Traditional interview are prone to bias and expenses to run an interview. With use of BART-MNLI, it prevents lacking qualitative depth of conversation while employing automated scoring based on required labels from open-ended answers. The research hyptohesized that \textit{there is a significant positive correlation between the semantic scores generated by the BART-MNLI model and the evaluation scores provided by expert human recruiters for the same set of open-ended responses.}

\par The primary aim of this reserach was to develop and make use of a simple python app that utilized Zero-Shot Natrual Language Processing to display real-time assesment of candidate personality traits based on open-ended responses.  Our research objectives were:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Implement BART-MNLI to generate a score for each user response
    \item Conduct correlation analysis between AI scores and human scores
    \item Measure and compare time taken to process automated scoring against time taken by human recruiters to evaluate users answers
    \item Conduct error analysis on instances with low correlation to detect linguistic issues or terms where ZSC fail to score.
\end{enumerate}

\par The research aimed to answer the following research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To what extent does the BART-MNLI zero-shot classification model correlate with expert human recruiter evaluations when scoring specific employer-required skills in open-ended interview responses?
    \item Does the use of automated NLI-based scoring significantly reduce the time required for the initial candidate screening phase compared to traditional human-led qualitative analysis?
    \item What are the primary linguistic limitations of the BART-MNLI model when interpreting nuanced or industry-specific terminology in candidate "stories"?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Onion}
\par This research used a positivist approach since the algorithm scored the user’s answers based on whether the response met the employer’s required personality traits or skills. 
\par The study followed a deductive approach, aimed to test the hypothesis that artificial intelligence (AI) could accurately match human-defined traits.
\par This was a case study that focused on validation of development and testing of interview prototype that used BART-MNLI, which aimed to score the answer based on labels (employer’s required personality traits). It used quantitative mono-method since it would focus on numerical score calculated by NLP model. This research was conducted in cross-sectional where data was collected at a single point in time. Data was collected via automated content analysis via BART-MNLI.

\subsection{Validity, Reliability, and Generalisability/Transferability}
\par BART-MNLI's potential role in personality assesment was validated by comparing scores with datasets between AI and human reviewers. A fixed random seed for BART-MNLI was used to validate inputs that always generate identical scores. 
\par Since BART-MNLI is a ZSC, it was designed to score based on labels specified without training. However, since the dataset is trained on general sources such as wikipedia, the dataset of user responses may contain industrial-specifc terms that are not understood by ZSC. 

\subsection{Ethical Considerations}
\par BART-MNLI is a pre trained from massive datasets such as Wikipedia which would include misinformation as well as morally wrong information. Due to bias, this could lead to a possibility of unfair scoring based on linguistic style. All sensitive information would be removed for the participants to remain anonymous. Finally, ZSC are known to give lack of reasoning behind scoring based on user response which leads to lack of transparency.
